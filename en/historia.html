Our story

A family home

Long before it welcomed guests, Casa da Tapada was already keeping our memories.

Our roots

My connection to Atalaia do Campo began long before Casa da Tapada existed.

Ever since I was a little girl, I came to the village to visit my grandparents. This is where I grew up, surrounded by family stories, nature and the freedom that comes from having roots in a place. Even while living in Lisbon, Atalaia was always part of my life: a place to return to, a place of belonging and of memories.

The old hay barn

As I grew up, my parents decided to buy a farm in a more rural part of the village, which had once belonged to my father's great-grandparents. The family kept growing and my grandparents' house was becoming too small for everyone. At the same time, we had started to prefer the silence of the countryside to the bustle and noise of the village centre.

On the farm there was an old hay barn, which my parents gradually adapted to our family's needs. It had no great luxuries or every comfort, but it had what we needed to escape the routine, be together and enjoy the peace of the countryside.

My children still lived many moments there, at a time when conditions were very simple. For us, that was never a problem. On the contrary, it was precisely that simplicity that allowed us to switch off, slow down and truly enjoy our days in the village. And it felt so good.

Later, as my children grew up, my parents decided to build a bigger house on the same farm — the one where we still stay whenever we visit them, which happens quite often, almost every weekend.

With the move to the new house, the old hay barn lost the role it had played for so many years and became mostly a storeroom, where my parents kept some of the produce they harvested from the land.

A new life

Over time, while at university, my daughter began bringing some friends along, boys and girls who fell in love with our village from the very first moment. Every year they came back for the village festivities and found there a place where they could be together, far from the routine and rush of the city.

That was when the house needed a new life. My parents gave it a small renovation and furnished it with pieces from other houses. Without great luxuries or concerns about style, it once again did what it had always done so well: welcome people, create happy moments and keep new memories.

In mid-2025, someone suggested that I turn the house into holiday accommodation. I admit that, at first, I was not very keen on the idea. As I had never felt the need to look for this kind of retreat myself, I found it hard to believe that other people might be looking for exactly what had always felt so natural to me.

Even so, I talked it over with the family and, little by little, we decided to take the plunge. My parents trusted me and gave me the house, allowing me to give it a new direction and make it whatever I imagined.

My husband and children supported me from the start, although with some reservations and doubts about whether the project would really work. Perhaps that is why this transformation began more as an experiment than as a certainty.

The outdoor area

Then the works began. I wanted to perfect the house to my taste, from the paintwork to the decoration, taking care of every detail so that the space would be cosy, beautiful and truly reflect who we are. By the end of that year, the house was ready.

Even so, for me something was missing. Our region has such a pleasant climate for much of the year that it felt important to create a space where guests could also enjoy the outdoors. That is how the idea of the leisure area came about.

I admit that, at the time, the proposal was not received with much enthusiasm. The family feared we would invest too much in a project that, if there was no demand, could end up abandoned. After all, we already had all those comforts on the farm.

But I felt it was different. I did not just want to adapt what already existed for our own use. I wanted to create something exclusive for those who chose to stay at Casa da Tapada — a space designed for resting, enjoying the warm days, being with family and experiencing the peace of the farm in privacy.

We let January and February go by and, in March, my husband decided to believe in this idea with me. We went ahead with the outdoor area and, by mid-April, everything was finally finished.

Today

That is when this adventure truly began. We launched the Casa da Tapada page on Instagram, with the whole space ready, and what followed was a real whirlwind of emotions. People started following the page, liking the project, asking for information and making bookings. The response exceeded all our expectations.

Even the most hesitant ended up admitting that the persistence had been worth it. I received many compliments for the good taste, the dedication and for believing in the idea even when there were still so many doubts.

Today, I am no longer the only one dreaming about this project. We are a team, a whole family working to welcome everyone who chooses Casa da Tapada in the best possible way.

And it is deeply rewarding to feel the warmth of those who visit us, to receive kind messages, to know they enjoyed their stay and to realise that this place, which kept our memories for so many years, has also become part of the memories of other families.

We look forward to welcoming you to Casa da Tapada.
Carla and family
